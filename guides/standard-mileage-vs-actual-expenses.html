Standard Mileage vs Actual Expenses for Rideshare

Educational guide · not tax advice. Confirm current figures at IRS.gov.

Rideshare drivers ask which car method “saves more.” The honest answer is that you figure both, using your miles and your bills, and you use a method only if the IRS lets you use it for that car.

Standard mileage

Multiply business miles by the rate for the date you drove them. For 2026 the IRS standard mileage rates are 72.5 cents through June 30 and 76 cents from July 1. Publication 463 says this rate stands in for depreciation or lease payments, maintenance and repairs, gasoline including gasoline taxes, oil, insurance, and registration. You cannot deduct those again.

You can still deduct business parking, tolls, and, if you are self-employed, the business percentage of car-loan interest.

IRS Topic 510 limits who may use the rate. You must own or lease the car. You cannot use the rate if you run five or more cars at the same time, if you depreciated the car with MACRS, section 179, or a special depreciation allowance, or if you already claimed actual expenses on a leased car. For a car you own, you choose the rate in the first year it is available for the business; after that you may change to actual expenses. For a lease, the rate has to stay in place for the entire lease including renewals. Publication 463 says you can elect the rate even if you used the car for hire, such as a taxi, unless one of those other bans applies.

Actual expenses

Add what you spent to operate the car and multiply by business miles ÷ total miles. Personal miles, including commuting, stay out. This method needs receipts. It can be larger when insurance, repairs, or depreciation are high and business miles are a large share of all miles. It can be smaller when the car is cheap to run and you drive a lot of business miles.

A split-year illustration

These miles are an example, not a claim about a typical Uber or Lyft driver. 5,000 business miles from January through June at 72.5 cents = $3,625. Another 5,000 from July through December at 76 cents = $3,800. Combined standard-mileage deduction = $7,425, plus any business tolls and parking. Actual expenses would be a different stack of receipts times the business-use percentage. Compare your stack. The mileage calculator does the standard-rate side only, so run it twice if your miles cross July 1.

Common questions

Can Uber drivers use the standard mileage rate?

Topic 510 and Publication 463 allow it when you meet the tests: you own or lease the car, you do not operate five or more cars at once, and you have not taken the depreciation choices that block the rate. A car used for hire can still elect the rate unless another ban applies.

Which method is bigger in 2026?

It depends on your receipts and your business-use percentage. The IRS tells you to figure both when you qualify for both. Nobody can rank them for every driver.

Do I apply 76 cents to January miles?

No. January through June 2026 stays at 72.5 cents. The 76-cent rate starts July 1, 2026.
