Quarterly Estimated Tax Deadlines for 2026

Educational guide · not tax advice. Confirm current figures at IRS.gov.

If nobody withholds tax from your 1099 work, the IRS generally wants the tax during the year. The 2026 Form 1040-ES tells calendar-year filers they can pay the whole estimate by April 15, 2026, or in four amounts by the dates below.

2026 due dates

  • Payment 1: April 15, 2026.
  • Payment 2: June 15, 2026.
  • Payment 3: September 15, 2026.
  • Payment 4: January 15, 2027.

You do not have to make the January 15, 2027 payment if you file your 2026 return by February 1, 2027, and pay the entire balance due with that return. The form says a mailed payment postmarked by the due date is treated as paid on the postmark date, and it warns that the USPS postmark is the date the payment is processed at a facility, which may not be the day you dropped it in a mailbox. Paying online avoids that question. If a due date lands on a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, the next business day counts. These four dates are the ones printed on the 2026 form.

The periods are not equal quarters. Form 1040-ES and the IRS estimated-tax FAQ use January 1–March 31, April 1–May 31, June 1–August 31, and September 1–December 31.

Who has to pay

Form 1040-ES says you must pay estimated tax for 2026 if you expect to owe at least $1,000 after subtracting withholding and refundable credits, and you expect withholding and refundable credits to be less than the smaller of 90 percent of your 2026 tax or 100 percent of your 2025 tax (110 percent if your 2025 AGI was more than $150,000, or $75,000 if married filing separately for 2026). Estimate self-employment tax and income tax on profit for the year, then decide how to split it. Dividing by four is the form’s “four equal amounts” path. Common questions

When are quarterly taxes due for gig workers in 2026?

Form 1040-ES lists April 15, 2026, June 15, 2026, September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027. The January payment can be skipped if you file the 2026 return by February 1, 2027 and pay the balance with it.

Are the four periods three months each?

No. The IRS periods end March 31, May 31, August 31, and December 31. The second period is two months long.

What if I only started dashing in the fall?

Form 1040-ES points you to the annualized income installment method in Publication 505 when income starts after March 31. You may still need Form 2210, Schedule AI, with the return.
