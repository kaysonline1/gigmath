Estimated Tax Safe Harbor for 1099 Freelancers

Educational guide · not tax advice. Confirm current figures at IRS.gov.

Estimated-tax penalties are about paying late or paying too little during the year, not about whether you get a refund in April. The 2026 Form 1040-ES states a general rule with two parts. You generally must pay estimated tax if both are true:

  1. You expect to owe at least $1,000 for 2026 after withholding and refundable credits.
  2. You expect withholding and refundable credits to be less than the smaller of 90 percent of the tax on your 2026 return, or 100 percent of the tax on your 2025 return. The 2025 return has to cover all 12 months.

That second test is the safe harbor. Meet the smaller of those two amounts, on time, and the underpayment penalty generally does not apply. Paying 100 percent of last year’s tax can be simpler when this year’s freelancing is uneven and you do not want to guess the full 2026 bill.

The higher-income swap

If your 2025 adjusted gross income was more than $150,000, or more than $75,000 if your 2026 filing status is married filing separately, Form 1040-ES says to use 110 percent instead of 100 percent of the prior-year tax. Farming and fishing have a different percentage. Most gig drivers are not in that special rule.

Withholding counts

A W-2 job’s withholding is part of the test. The form says you can ask an employer to withhold more on Form W-4, which can cover the 1099 side without four separate payments. Withholding is treated as paid through the year. Estimated payments have to hit the installment dates: April 15, 2026, June 15, 2026, September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027. Details are in the 2026 deadlines guide.

What the safe harbor is not

It does not set your DoorDash or freelance tax rate. It does not replace Schedule C. Figure the likely tax with the self-employment tax calculator, and if you sell your time to clients rather than to an app, price that time with the freelance hourly rate calculator so the set-aside is in the rate you charge.

Common questions

What is the federal estimated-tax safe harbor for 2026?

Form 1040-ES: withholding and credits at least equal to the smaller of 90 percent of your 2026 tax or 100 percent of your 2025 tax. Use 110 percent of the 2025 tax if 2025 AGI was over $150,000, or over $75,000 if you are married filing separately for 2026.

I had no tax last year. Do I still pay quarterly?

The form’s exception: if you were a U.S. citizen or resident for all of 2025 and had no tax liability for that full year, you do not have to pay 2026 estimated tax. Zero total tax, or no filing requirement, is what the form means by no liability.

Does the $1,000 rule mean I can ignore smaller bills?

You look at whether you expect to owe at least $1,000 after withholding and refundable credits. Owing $900 at filing time is outside that first test. Owing $1,000 or more can still require estimates if you also miss the 90 percent / prior-year test.
