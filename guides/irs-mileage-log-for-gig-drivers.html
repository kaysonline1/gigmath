IRS Mileage Log Rules for Gig Drivers

Educational guide · not tax advice. Confirm current figures at IRS.gov.

A mileage app total with no detail is not what the IRS describes as an adequate record. Publication 463 says you should keep adequate records or enough evidence to support your own statement, and that a written record is what usually counts. A record you type on a computer can qualify. A reconstruction months later is harder to defend.

The fields that match the publication

For car expenses, Publication 463’s record table asks for:

  • the date you used the car,
  • the mileage for each business use,
  • your business destination,
  • the business purpose,
  • and the total miles for the year.

Also keep proof of ownership or the lease. For actual expenses, keep receipts that show the amount and which vehicle they belong to. The standard mileage rate still needs the mileage record. It does not need a gas receipt for every fill-up, because gas is inside the rate.

Commuting has to be visible in the log

Mark miles that are commuting so they do not get multiplied by the IRS rate. Publication 463 treats the drive from home to your first business stop, and from the last stop home, as commuting when you have no office that qualifies as your principal place of business. Miles from one customer to the next are the business miles. Read which miles count before you export a file that starts the moment you leave home.

2026 rates to apply to the business miles

72.5 cents per business mile through June 30, 2026, and 76 cents from July 1, 2026. Your log needs dates so you can split the year. Then use the mileage deduction calculator. A blank weekly sheet is on the free mileage log page if you want paper.

Common questions

What must a gig driver mileage log include?

Publication 463 calls for the date, the miles for each business use, the destination, the business purpose, and total miles for the year, plus proof you own or lease the car.

Is a once-a-month odometer photo enough?

The publication wants the mileage for each business use, not only a monthly total. A sample of weeks can support a business-use percentage when it truly represents the rest of the month, which the publication illustrates, but a single photo is not a trip log.

Do I need gas receipts if I use the standard mileage rate?

The rate replaces gasoline. You still need the mileage record. Keep toll and business-parking receipts, because those are deducted separately.
